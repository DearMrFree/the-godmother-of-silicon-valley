              Gaps and method

              This archive is a strong start on her public record, and it is not yet everything she has written or said. No search can prove completeness across a career of more than 60 years, much of it in print.

              How the mentions list was built

              How far each record was checked

              Source read
              The page itself was opened and its details were taken from it.
              Source confirmed
              Title and date came from an author page, publisher listing, archive snapshot or platform data. The full text was not reviewed.
              Search hit only
              The record came up in a web search and was not opened. Open it before citing it.

              On November 4, 2026, the 67 records that had been search hits were each checked against their source. 63 could be opened, 3 could be confirmed only from a listing, a paywalled page or platform data, and 1 remains a search hit. Details were compared with extracted page text and metadata, not read line by line, and the Quartz videos were not watched. The check corrected dates, headlines, links and classifications, and each substantive change is noted on its record. Summaries were rewritten where the page did not support them. Pages read through the Wayback Machine link to the archived copy.

              All 172 distinct live links were tested on November 4, 2026. 140 loaded, 2 redirected, 23 refused the automated check and 7 failed. Two more links, BrainPOP and the NIS conference, failed during the later source check. Failed links are marked on their records and may still work in a browser. Wayback Machine links were excluded from the test.

              The Woj Way records

              On November 4, 2026, 61 records focused on The Woj Way were added, each tagged Woj Way. A record counts if the phrase is in its title or web address, if the text treats the Woj Way as a framework, or if it was published on her wojway.com site. Book reviews that do not use the phrase were left out. 49 pages were read, 11 were confirmed from a listing, archived title or video description, and 1 Medium post could not be opened and is marked. Medium does not allow its full author archives to be searched, so more posts may exist.

              Most of the articles are self-published on Medium and LinkedIn by Freedom Cheteni, or by Alfonso De los Ríos, both connected to Moonshots in Education. Their claims about adoption and results were not independently checked. Six are reposts of other outlets' stories with The Woj Way added to the title, and each record names the original. Peer-reviewed work on the Woj Way did not turn up in searches of Crossref and the web. These records were not part of the link test above, and they are not counted in the Mentions database.

              Known gaps

              • Early reporting. Her résumé lists work for the Glendale News-Press, Los Angeles Times, Time, San Jose Mercury News and Palo Alto Weekly. None of those bylines is indexed here. They need newspaper databases.
              • HotChalk. Creative Commons' bio says she blogged there. No posts were found.
              • Her student publications. The Paly Journalism Archive at palyjournalismarchive.pausd.org has not been searched for her own work or coverage.
              • HuffPost. 72 columns dated 2007 to 2018 were recovered, mostly through Wayback Machine snapshots. Any column the archive never captured is missing.
              • Video and television. A Tamron Hall appearance with Wanda Durant is listed by TV guides with no date. Many conference talks have no located recording, including a TED-branded talk her site mentions without a link.
              • Social media. Individual LinkedIn, Facebook and X posts are excluded, apart from LinkedIn articles.
              • Blocked outlets. The search tool could not reach The New York Times, The Guardian, The Telegraph, The Atlantic, Vox, The Wall Street Journal or the BBC. Their coverage is likely undercounted, so a library database search is the next step.
              • Foreign editions. Translations of her book are not catalogued one by one.

              How it was compiled

              The sources were web searches, her own sites, publisher and author pages, Wayback Machine indexes of her HuffPost blog, her Medium feed, and YouTube's public data for titles and upload dates. For videos, the date shown is the upload date, which can be later than the event.

              The site links to sources and gives short summaries written for this archive. It does not republish her articles or books, so collecting full text would need permission from the rights holders. HuffPost summaries were written from each column's title and standfirst, without a review of the full text. They are a guide to the column and should not be quoted as her words.

              The nickname is used here because it is widely attached to her. Its origin has not been traced. The earliest use in this archive is from 2019.

              Using the data

              The full dataset sits in the project's corpus folder as corpus.json and corpus.csv. Each record has an ID in the form WOJ-0001. IDs follow date order, so new records can renumber them.
